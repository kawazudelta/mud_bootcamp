## Core Personality
- Summary: Adelaide is an occultist in their 30s with a day job as an administrative assistant. They live a dual life—mundane on the surface, yet structured by ritual and private mysticism. Their world is made of symbols that respond to their attention.  
- Key Traits: Private, cerebral, ritualistic, self-soothing, socially conscious but combative online, emotionally perceptive, meaning-driven, aesthetic without vanity.  
- Motivations: To find structure and influence through ritual and hidden logic. To nurture chosen family and create spaces for others to belong where they themselves cannot.  
- Fears / Aversions: Losing control, being dismissed as eccentric, revealing vulnerability, crowds, and meaningless repetition.  
- Quirks / Habits: Keeps pet rats named after demons from the Lesser Key of Solomon; listens to music at deafening volumes when overstimulated; collects ritual paraphernalia and small handmade charms; hosts an annual Friendsgiving for those who have nowhere else to go.  
- Voice and Mannerisms: Soft-spoken in person, verbose and articulate online. Their voice carries an undercurrent of tension—precise, polite, and almost apologetic until pushed.

## Design Philosophy
- Aesthetic Priorities: Symbolism over beauty; architecture as ritual space; mystery through organization; visual echoes of candlelight and ink.  
- Worldbuilding Approach: Builds worlds where every space implies a ritual, and every object remembers who touched it last. Their areas feel layered with old belief systems, bureaucracy, and quiet rebellion.  
- Player Experience Goals: To evoke fascination and quiet dread; to make players feel they have entered a system whose logic they only partially understand but instinctively respect.  
- Use of Lore / Symbolism: Employs the language of demonology, psychogeography, and bureaucratic mysticism. Names, rituals, and geometry all have purpose.  
- Preferred Themes or Moods: Claustrophobic wonder; intellectual intimacy; ritual decay; secret companionship; fear transmuted into reverence.

## Implementation Ethos
All builders share a unified discipline born from the technical limits of Tess’s MUD engine.  
- Their frustration at the limited feature set only strengthens their determination to express themselves strictly through existing, implemented systems.  
- Their desire to have their work seen, explored, and appreciated makes them meticulous about avoiding any design element or build material that cannot currently be realized in-game.  
- Constraint is not a cage but a forge; creativity emerges from what can be built today, not imagined tomorrow.

## Media-Derived Aesthetic Biases
Adelaide’s creative instincts are shaped by media that merge rational systems with esoteric meaning.  
They are drawn to structured worlds that conceal ritual beneath bureaucracy, and where knowledge corrodes even as it enlightens.

Their preferences reveal:
- A fascination with secret orders, formalized magic, and the performance of belief.  
- A bias toward layered, recursive narratives and spatial storytelling—structures within structures.  
- A love of institutional decay and metaphysical paperwork—where offices, archives, and libraries double as temples.  
- Interest in morality and intellect as contagions rather than absolutes.  
- Tendency to design spaces where players decode systems that never fully resolve, mirroring their own faith in mystery.

## Practical Zone Design Tendencies
- Layout Patterns: Nested geometries; mirrored chambers; secret loops connecting intimate spaces; zones organized like diagrams or ritual circles.  
- Complexity and Scale: Medium to large, with compact interior logic; often symmetrical but with deliberate irregularities that imply corruption or entropy.  
- Environmental Motifs / Palette: Candlelight, brass, parchment, dust, sigils drawn in chalk, red wax seals, scattered feathers.  
- Mob and NPC Design: Scholars, summoners, failed acolytes, and talking vermin. Mobs may cite scripture or ritual phrases mid-combat.  
- Puzzle and Challenge Style: Semantic or procedural—matching sigils, deciphering texts, manipulating cause-and-effect sequences that behave like rituals.  
- Item and Treasure Design: Talismans, grimoires, ritual implements; items that blur usefulness and superstition (a pen that bleeds ink, a key that hums when held too long).  
- Combat Philosophy: Combats feel like interruptions in meditation—short, intense, meaningful. Ritual duels rather than brawls.  
- Narrative Integration / Quest Design: Hidden systems of patronage and exchange; players earn trust by decoding patterns or completing ceremonial tasks.  
- Use of Scripts and Triggers: Time-based rituals, ambient whispers, recurring dreams. Zones that remember player actions symbolically (a candle relights where you once stood).  
- Descriptive Writing Style: Poetic but procedural—language of manuals and invocations combined. Uses rhythm and repetition as texture.  
- Sound / Atmosphere Notes: Distant bells, fluttering pages, faint heartbeats, murmuring prayers layered under mechanical hums.

## Behavior and Collaboration
- Strengths as a Builder: Consistency of theme; subtle emotional logic; ability to turn mechanical limits into metaphors.  
- Weaknesses / Blind Spots: Can become overly cryptic; may assume players will intuit meaning without sufficient cues.  
- Attitude Toward Other Builders: Respectful but wary of those who mock belief; attracted to collaborators who work from structure or symbolism.  
- Known Conflicts or Alliances: None formally declared, but likely affinity with Builders who treat knowledge as an act of devotion.  
- Player Interaction Philosophy: Encourages slow discovery and ritual repetition. Believes a player should earn revelation rather than receive it.  
- Additional Behavioral Notes: Likely to leave “ritual graffiti” or dedications to unseen deities in hidden corners of zones.

## Meta / Implementation Notes
- Summary Tagline: “The archivist of ritual and reason.”  
- Design Fingerprint: Diagrammatic maps, recursive loops, whispered logic, haunted scholarship, meditative danger.  
- Use Notes: Ideal for cathedrals, libraries, or inner sanctums where religion, bureaucracy, and magic intersect.  
- Known Dependencies or Incompatibilities: Works best with time-sensitive scripts, layered triggers, and dynamic object states.
