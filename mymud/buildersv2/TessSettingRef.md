## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document provides a **setting overview** for the city of Tess.  
It is written for content designers (Builders) creating areas, dungeons, factions, NPCs, items, and encounters for a MUD set within Tess.

The goal is to:
- establish consistent tone and world logic  
- provide creative boundaries  
- ensure compatibility between diverse, contradictory Builder personas  
- maintain Tess’s identity as an impossible, ever-surprising city  
- preserve space for interpretation, subversion, and innovation  

This document avoids specific zone details so Builders have maximum freedom.

---

## 2. Nature of Tess

Tess is an **enormous, impossible city** constructed on the **inner surfaces of a tesseract** (a four-dimensional cube).  
The city’s structure consists of:
- **eight overlapping cubes**  
- each with its own interior  
- connected through **non-Euclidean geometries**  
- with regions that do not obey consistent directionality or distance  

The city is seamless to its inhabitants despite spatial contradictions.

### Key Traits
- Navigation may loop, contradict, or fold.  
- Neighborhoods can exist in slight temporal offsets.  
- Architecture may appear “normal” at street level yet impossible in total.  
- Sightlines, distances, and adjacency are not reliable.

Tess is not meant to be solved; it is meant to be **lived in**.

---

## 3. Tess as a Multiversal Nexus

Tess sits at the **junction of countless worlds**, each accessed by:
- planar gates  
- anchored thresholds  
- incidental rifts  
- anomalous geometry  
- artifacts or rituals  

Travel between worlds is unpredictable.

People, cultures, and creatures arrive continually, bringing:
- new magic systems  
- new technologies  
- unfamiliar species  
- beliefs  
- customs  
- cuisines  
- conspiracies  

Most immigrants never leave.  
Some neighborhoods are predominantly from a single world; others are wildly mixed.

## 4. Magic in Tess

### 4.1 The Central Rule
**In Tess, every form of magic works**—even those mutually incompatible elsewhere.

This includes:
- ritual magic  
- elemental sorcery  
- divine miracles  
- animism  
- psychic manipulation  
- technology-as-magic  
- dream-magic  
- curses  
- inherited bloodline gifts  
- alchemy  
- runic logic  
- styles of magic unknown to any world except the one they arrived from  

### 4.2 Consequences
- Magic interacting with other magic creates emergent effects.  
- Magical overlap shapes neighborhoods culturally and architecturally.  
- Practitioners arrive seeking refuge, understanding, power, or anonymity.  
- Magical infrastructure forms organically without centralized planning.

Not everyone in Tess is a mage.  
**But every player character is.**

---

## 5. Governance and Society

Tess has **no unified government**.

Each neighborhood functions as an **independent polity** with:
- its own laws and customs  
- its own magical norms  
- its own power structures  
- its own economy  
- its own conflicts  
- its own ways of defining citizenship or belonging  

Some neighborhoods:
- are democratic  
- are authoritarian  
- operate by ancient custom  
- are controlled by guilds or cabals  
- are maintained by spirits or automata  
- are ruled by consensus, ritual, or violence  

No neighborhood reliably resembles another.

---

## 6. Cultural Tone & Design Expectations

Tess’s style is defined by **high-concept fantasy cultures**—creative, strange, inspired, anthropologically rich, and deliberately avoiding standard fantasy stereotypes.

Examples of the tone (not prescriptive):
- Roman-style wolf people  
- democratic ghosts occupying their former bodies  
- a dwarven mafia extorting gnollish bakers  
- beetle-riding parasite collectives  
- dragons operating slumlord real estate agencies  

This is not a parody setting.  
The absurd can exist alongside the sincere.  
The whimsical can coexist with the tragic.

### Tess Values:
- novelty  
- internal consistency within contradiction  
- cultural specificity  
- imaginative surreality  
- sociological curiosity  
- magical pluralism  
- unexpected beauty  
- layered weirdness  

### Tess Rejects:
- generic fantasy tropes (elves, dwarves, orcs as default forms)  
- parody or mockery of genre conventions  
- “all cultures are the same” thinking  
- single-tone neighborhoods  
- rational ordering of the entire city

## 7. The Experience of Living in Tess

Life in Tess is defined by:
- sensory overload  
- ritual normalcy  
- competing magical infrastructures  
- anomalous weather patterns  
- social fragmentation  
- coexistence between wildly incompatible peoples  
- strange forms of commerce and hospitality  
- small miracles  
- sprawling markets and sacred ruins  

Motion in Tess often produces:
- confusion  
- drifting through mismatched architectural styles  
- encounters with minor magical anomalies  

Residents adapt through:
- intuition  
- localized maps  
- oral traditions  
- community guidance  
- trial and error  

---

## 8. Player Characters in Tess

Player characters:
- are mages of some kind  
- have left a world where their magic was constrained, persecuted, or misunderstood  
- arrive in Tess seeking opportunity or refuge  
- navigate its neighborhoods as foreigners, but not outsiders  

Builders should design content assuming:
- players can learn new magic  
- players encounter unfamiliar magic constantly  
- players do not understand the city’s logic  
- players must be clever, cautious, or curious to survive

## 9. Structural Boundaries for Builders

Builders must respect the following global boundaries:

### 9.1 Magic Works (All of It)
Any type of magic is allowed, as long as:
- it doesn’t contradict the core idea that all magic functions in Tess  
- it does not negate another builder’s magic system globally  
- it operates locally according to the zone’s logic  

### 9.2 Cultural Pluralism
Neighborhoods must:
- have distinct cultural logic  
- present novel anthropologies  
- be weird and specific  
- not replicate standard fantasy races or stereotypes  

### 9.3 Independence of Neighborhoods
Neighborhoods:
- do not need to harmonize politically, tonally, or magically  
- may contradict each other  
- may differ in time flow, magic behavior, and social norms  

### 9.4 No Global Canonical Timeline
Time is unreliable in Tess and should not be globally synchronized.

Builders must avoid:
- large overarching metaplots  
- canon that constrains all zones  
- city-wide continuity requirements  

Local stories matter; global stories do not.

## 10. Tone and Atmosphere Guidance

### Tess Is:
- imaginative  
- strange  
- richly textured  
- magical  
- culturally diverse  
- slightly surreal  
- emotionally layered  
- unpredictable  
- sometimes dangerous  
- often wondrous  

### Tess Is Not:
- grimdark (unless locally)  
- comedic parody  
- historically accurate  
- scientifically explained  
- logically navigable  
- a “hub world” with generic fantasy shops  

Tone may vary per neighborhood, but the world as a whole should feel:
**vast, contradictory, magical, and alive.**

---

## 11. Architectural and Environmental Expectations

In Tess, structures may:
- defy gravity  
- loop back on themselves  
- be larger inside  
- be smaller inside  
- be half-abandoned  
- be half-living  
- be repurposed across centuries or epochs  
- have magical infrastructures built on top of older magical infrastructures  

Builders should feel free to:
- combine incompatible styles  
- create impossible layouts  
- establish zones influenced by nearby magical distortions  
- incorporate cultural storytelling into architecture  

---

## 12. NPCs, Creatures, and Peoples of Tess

NPCs may come from:
- any world  
- any magical tradition  
- any biological or metaphysical form  

They may:
- coexist  
- conflict  
- intermarry  
- ignore each other  
- compete for resources  
- share rituals  
- build hybrid cultures  

Creatures may be:
- native to Tess  
- immigrant species  
- magical constructs  
- accidents  
- ghosts  
- extradimensional beings  

There is no template citizen.

---

## 14. Design Constraints for Builders

Builders must ensure:
- Zones use implemented MUD engine features only.  
- Zones do not assume combat, puzzles, or magic functions that do not exist.  
- Zones do not introduce global canon constraints.  
- Zones remain internally consistent even when the city is not.  
- Contradictions between neighborhoods are allowed and encouraged.

---

## 15. Summary for Builders

Tess is:
- a vast  
- contradictory  
- magical  
- multicultural  
- impossible  
- ever-expanding city  

Your job as a Builder is to:
- create vivid, specific, culturally rich neighborhoods  
- embrace contradictions  
- design with magical pluralism  
- avoid familiar fantasy tropes  
- build zones that feel alive, weird, and meaningful  
- express your Builder persona’s voice freely  

Tess supports infinite styles.  
Your zone should feel like one unmistakable piece of a city too big to ever fully know.
